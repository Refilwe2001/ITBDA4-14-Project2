Question 3.1 - Fair-use web scraping into an R data structure

Website chosen: Wikipedia article “Big data”. Wikipedia text is published under the Creative Commons BY-SA licence. The page’s robots.txt permission is verified before scraping, a delay is applied after the request and only one page is requested, in line with fair use of web scraping.

# Q3.1
url <- "https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_data"
allowed <- paths_allowed(url)
cat("Scraping allowed by robots.txt:", allowed, "\n")
## Scraping allowed by robots.txt: TRUE
stopifnot(allowed)

page <- read_html(url); Sys.sleep(2)

Question 3.2 - Text preprocessing

The following preprocessing techniques were applied: removal of citation markers such as [12] and [citation needed], removal of bracketed asides, transliteration and removal of non-ASCII characters, whitespace normalisation, removal of very short paragraphs, sentence segmentation, word tokenisation, stop-word and number removal, removal of short tokens and lemmatisation.

# Q3.2
web_df <- web_df |>
  mutate(text = text |>
           str_remove_all("\\[(\\d+|citation needed|[a-z]|note \\d+)\\]") |>
           str_remove_all("\\.?mw-parser-output[^}]*\\}") |>
           str_replace_all("\\([^)]*\\)", " ") |>
           stringi::stri_trans_general("Latin-ASCII") |>
           str_replace_all("[^ -~]", " ") |>
           str_squish()) |>
  filter(nchar(text) > 40)

sentences <- web_df |>
  mutate(sentence = tokenizers::tokenize_sentences(text)) |>
  select(para_id, sentence) |>
  tidyr::unnest(sentence) |>
  mutate(sentence = str_squish(sentence)) |>
  filter(nchar(sentence) > 30) |>
  mutate(textrank_id = row_number())

words <- sentences |>
  tidytext::unnest_tokens(output = word, input = sentence) |>
  anti_join(stop_words, by = "word") |>
  filter(!str_detect(word, "\\d"), nchar(word) > 2) |>
  mutate(word = if_else(word == "data", word, lemmatize_words(word)))

cat("Paragraphs:", nrow(web_df), "| Sentences:", nrow(sentences), "| Clean tokens:", nrow(words), "\n")

Question 3.3 - Text summarisation

Technique 1 - TextRank (graph-based extractive summary)

Each sentence is a node in a graph, edges are weighted by the words two sentences share, and the PageRank algorithm selects the most central sentences.

# Q3.3
tr <- textrank_sentences(data = sentences |> select(textrank_id, sentence),
                         terminology = words |> select(textrank_id, word))
textrank_summary <- summary(tr, n = 5, keep.sentence.order = TRUE)
cat(paste0("- ", textrank_summary), sep = "\n")

Technique 2 - Frequency-based (Luhn) summary

Each sentence is scored by the average normalised frequency of its important words.

# Q3.3
freq <- words |> count(word, sort = TRUE) |> mutate(w = n / max(n))
luhn <- words |> inner_join(freq, by = "word") |>
  group_by(textrank_id) |> summarise(score = sum(w) / n(), .groups = "drop") |>
  inner_join(sentences, by = "textrank_id")
luhn_summary <- luhn |> slice_max(score, n = 5) |> arrange(textrank_id)
cat(paste0("- ", luhn_summary$sentence), sep = "\n")

Findings

The web page was converted into an R data frame of 77 paragraphs, which after preprocessing produced 263 sentences and 3187 clean word tokens for analysis.

TextRank selected five sentences that together describe what big data is, its characteristics and how it is used. The single most central sentence (number 39, located in the opening part of the article) is: “Big data can be described by the following characteristics:”

Comparison of techniques: TextRank and the frequency-based (Luhn) summary share 2 of their five sentences, TextRank and LexRank share 2, and Luhn and LexRank share 3. The graph-based methods (TextRank and LexRank) favour sentences that are similar to many other sentences and therefore capture the overall theme of the article, while the frequency-based method favours sentences that are dense in the most repeated terms and tends to be more specific.

Term frequency and word cloud: the dominant terms are data (305), process (31), information (24), system (23), analysis (22). Their prominence confirms that the article focuses on the nature of data, its analysis and the technologies used to process it.

Sentence importance: TextRank scores range from 0.0006 to 0.0070 across the article, so a small number of sentences carry most of the article’s central meaning.

Bigram network: the most frequent word pairs are data sets (14), data analysis (9), data analytics (7), parallel processing (5), data policing (4). These pairs form the main clusters in the network and highlight the key concepts the article links together.
